Energy meters measure electricity flow at various points in your installation and form the basis for intelligent control by evcc.

## Usage areas \{#usage-areas\}

Each device on this page can be used in one or more of the following roles:

{/* prettier-ignore */}
<ul class="usage-list">
  <li><span class="tag tag-usages"><UsageIcons usages={["grid"]} /></span> <strong>Grid meter</strong> (<code>grid</code>): central grid connection</li>
  <li><span class="tag tag-usages"><UsageIcons usages={["pv"]} /></span> <strong>Solar production</strong> (<code>pv</code>): energy producer</li>
  <li><span class="tag tag-usages"><UsageIcons usages={["battery"]} /></span> <strong>Battery</strong> (<code>battery</code>): home battery</li>
  <li><span class="tag tag-usages"><UsageIcons usages={["charge"]} /></span> <strong>Charger meter</strong> (<code>charge</code>): additional meter at a charging point</li>
  <li><span class="tag tag-usages"><UsageIcons usages={["aux"]} /></span> <strong>Self-regulating consumer</strong> (<code>aux</code>): consumer with its own surplus regulation</li>
</ul>

Hybrid inverters often serve multiple roles at once — typically solar production, battery status, and sometimes the grid connection.
In that case you reference the same device at every relevant role.
