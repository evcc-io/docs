We are often asked which devices work well with evcc.
On this page we've collected what to look for when buying a [charger](#chargers), a [solar or battery system](#energy), a [meter](#meters), a [smart switch](#smartswitches), a [heat pump](#heatpumps) or a [vehicle](#vehicles).

:::tip[Hardware to run evcc]
If you're looking for a computer to run evcc on, see the [hardware page](/en/installation/hardware).
:::

A charger that can be controlled is what makes smart charging possible.
See the [complete list of supported chargers](/en/chargers).
A few points are worth checking before you buy:

<NavCardGrid>
  <NavCard
    check
    title="Avoid cloud-only chargers"
    description="Chargers like Easee, Nexblue, Volt Time or Zaptec can only be controlled through the manufacturer's cloud. Charging then depends on your internet connection and the manufacturer's cloud infrastructure."
  />
  <NavCard
    check
    title="Prefer Ethernet connection"
    description="Weak WiFi at the charger is the most frequent complaint in the community and a common source of unwanted behaviour. Prefer a model with an Ethernet port."
  />

<NavCard check title="Phase switching">

We recommend a charger with automatic [phase switching](/en/chargers?f=1p3p#supported).
With little solar surplus, e.g. in winter, it charges on one phase, and with a lot of it on three.

</NavCard>

<NavCard
  check
  title="Check hidden costs"
  description="Some chargers accept external control only with a paid licence from the manufacturer or in a certain model variant. Check the device page before you buy."
/>

<NavCard check title="Built-in meter">

We recommend a charger with a [built-in meter](/en/chargers?f=meter#supported).
It makes your statistics more accurate, because without one the charged energy has to be estimated.

</NavCard>

</NavCardGrid>

Solar production and the charge level of your home battery are read from the inverter or battery system.
Many systems also allow the battery to be controlled.
See the [complete list of supported systems](/en/meters).

<NavCardGrid>
  <NavCard
    check
    title="Avoid cloud-only systems"
    description="Values read through the manufacturer's cloud are often minutes old. Ask your installer to enable the local interface, e.g. Modbus TCP, when the system is set up."
  />

<NavCard check title="Battery control">

Holding the home battery back or charging it from the grid needs [active battery control](/en/features/battery#battery-control).

</NavCard>

<NavCard check title="Grid operator control">

In some countries, e.g. Germany, you might be obliged to let the grid operator reduce your consumption or feed-in.
Look for [dimmable](/en/meters?f=dim#supported) and [curtailable](/en/meters?f=curtail#supported) devices and read [External Limit](/en/external-limit).

</NavCard>

<NavCard info title="Energy resolution">

Some inverters and batteries report energy only in steps of 0.1 kWh or 1 kWh, which makes statistics for short periods imprecise.
Check the **Known limitations** on the device page.

</NavCard>

</NavCardGrid>

Quirks such as connection limits, slow values or required licences are documented on the device pages, so look up the exact model before you decide.

## Energy Meters \{#meters\}

<IconBanner
  icons={[
    "meters/shelly-pro-em",
    "meters/eastron-sdm630-modbus",
    "meters/kostal-smart-energy-meter",
    "meters/homewizard-wi-fi-p1-meter",
    "meters/discovergy",
    "meters/abb-b23",
  ]}
/>

Solar surplus charging needs the power at the grid connection point.
A charger without a built-in meter or a heat pump can get a meter of its own, which makes the statistics more accurate.

<NavCardGrid>
  <NavCard
    check
    title="Often already included"
    description="Most solar and battery systems already measure the power at the grid connection point. Check this first, because then no further meter is needed."
  />
  <NavCard
    check
    title="Wired meter"
    description="A wired meter installed in the meter cabinet by an electrician is the most reliable option."
  />

<NavCard check title="P1 and HAN port">

In the Netherlands, Belgium and Luxembourg smart meters have a [P1 port](/en/meters?q=p1#supported), and in Scandinavia a similar [HAN port](/en/meters?q=han#supported).
A dongle on this port delivers the grid power locally.
In some countries the grid operator has to enable the port first.

</NavCard>

<NavCard cross title="Smart meter gateway">

In Germany a smart meter gateway has an Ethernet port, but it does not supply usable power and energy data.
An alternative is to add an infrared read head to the meter itself (_moderne Messeinrichtung_).

</NavCard>

<NavCard check title="Shelly meters">

<ExternalLinks>

Meters from [Shelly](https://www.shelly.com/) are popular in smart homes and a good option, also for single devices.
Most of them are connected via WiFi.

</ExternalLinks>

</NavCard>

</NavCardGrid>

<ExternalLinks>

Other widely used brands are [ABB](https://global.abb/), [Carlo Gavazzi](https://www.carlogavazzi.com/), [Chint](https://www.chint.com/) and [Eastron](https://www.eastroneurope.com/).
Most of their meters are connected via RS485.

</ExternalLinks>

## Heat Pumps \{#heatpumps\}

<IconBanner
  icons={[

A heat pump can run in boost operation when there is solar surplus or cheap energy, and be reduced when the grid operator demands it.
See the [complete list of supported heating devices](/en/heating).

<NavCardGrid>
  <NavCard
    check
    title="SG Ready"
    description="Nearly all heat pumps have an SG Ready input. A relay such as a Shelly is enough to request boost operation, even if the heat pump has no other interface."
  />
  <NavCard
    check
    title="Prefer a local interface"
    description="Look for a device with a local interface such as Modbus TCP or EEBUS. Some heat pumps, e.g. from Viessmann, can only be controlled via cloud."
  />
<NavCard check title="Separate meter">

Plan a [meter](#meters) at the power supply of the heat pump.
The values a heat pump reports itself are estimates on many models.

</NavCard>

<NavCard check title="Grid operator control">

In some countries, e.g. Germany, you might be obliged to let the grid operator reduce the power of your heat pump.
Look for [dimmable](/en/heating?f=dim#supported) devices.

</NavCard>

</NavCardGrid>

Most brands with restrictions use the unofficial API of the manufacturer's app, which can stop working without notice.

1. Access is based on the EU Data Act and therefore available in Europe only.
2. Requires a free developer account.
3. Workaround via the EU Data Act web portal. An official interface is [announced](https://github.com/evcc-io/evcc/discussions/36190) for 2027.

Our [list of vehicles](/en/vehicles) has the details for each brand.

:::caution[Ask for your vehicle data]
We believe that owners should be able to access this data directly and free of charge, without a third-party service.
The EU Data Act backs this up, yet many manufacturers do not comply.
Sign the [petition](/en/blog/2025/12/18/eu-data-act-petition) and **ask your manufacturer directly**.
Škoda shows that feedback from real customers helps, see the [blog post](/en/blog/2026/08/27/skoda-vehicle-api).
:::

### Alternatives \{#alternatives\}

If your brand is not supported or its connection is unreliable, the charge level can also come from another source.

<NavCardGrid>

<NavCard check title="OBD dongle">

A dongle in the diagnostic port reads the charge level locally and sends it over WiFi.
Examples are [Open Vehicle Monitoring System](https://www.openvehicles.com/), [evNotify](https://evnotify.de/) and [WiCAN Pro](https://www.meatpi.com/).

</NavCard>

<NavCard check title="ISO 15118">

A charger with [ISO 15118](/en/chargers?f=iso151182#supported) can read the charge level over the charging cable.
We like the idea, but so far it works with few combinations, and only at the start of a session or by interrupting charging.

</NavCard>

<NavCard check title="Third-party services">

[Tronity](/en/vehicles/tronity) connects to many brands and passes the data on.
A vehicle that is connected in the app of [Tibber](/en/vehicles/tibber) or Octopus Energy ([Germany](/en/vehicles/octopus-energy-germany), [Italy](/en/vehicles/octopus-energy-italy)) can be read from there as well.

</NavCard>

<NavCard check title="Via other open source projects">

Data that another project already collects can be reused, e.g. from [Home Assistant](/en/vehicles/home-assistant), TeslaMate, PSA Car Controller or ioBroker.
A Tesla can also be read locally via Bluetooth.
See our [generic support](/en/vehicles#generic) section.

</NavCard>

</NavCardGrid>

Without any charge level you can still set the [charging limit](/en/features/limits) in kWh or add the vehicle [offline](/en/features/vehicle) with its battery capacity.
